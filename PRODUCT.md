# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Delegated: Astro, generating static HTML. It needs no runtime, deploys free to GitHub Pages, Vercel, or Netlify, and uses Markdown content collections for project and paper entries, so new entries need no code edits. No deploy target has been chosen yet.

## Users

The main audience is recruiters and hiring managers looking at an internship or job candidate. They often arrive from a resume or application link and give it a quick look, frequently under a minute. They need to judge fit fast, then decide whether to dig into specific work or reach out.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Adithya Gnanasundar (UCLA). It shows software projects and research or papers so that hiring decision-makers can confirm skills and judgment beyond the resume. Success means a reviewer quickly understands who Adithya is and what they've built, and can reach the resume, the code, and contact info without friction.

## Positioning

It pairs shipped software with research work. The site should show both building and rigorous thinking. The specific claims come from the real projects and papers. Don't write generic "passionate developer" copy.

## Operating Context

- Visitors come from application portals, LinkedIn, email, and resume links, on desktop and phone.
- Reviewers move between this site, the resume PDF, and GitHub.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Sections: about or intro, software projects (with repo links), research or papers, resume or CV access, and contact.
- Content should be easy to update over time by adding entries.
- Undecided: domain, deploy target, whether to include a blog.

## Evidence on Hand

The user has, but hasn't yet provided:
- Resume or CV
- Project write-ups and repositories
- Headshot and images or screenshots

Don't invent projects, metrics, publications, employers, awards, or testimonials. Until the real content is supplied, use clearly marked placeholders.

## Product Principles

1. Make fit clear within seconds. The most important information goes first.
2. Back every claim with evidence: a link to a repo, a paper, or a demo.
3. Keep the resume, GitHub, and contact one action away from anywhere.
4. Keep content in structured entries so the site grows without redesign.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA. The site must stay fully usable on mobile and with a keyboard.
